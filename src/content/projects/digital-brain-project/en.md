How does the human brain reason, plan and decide in complex situations? AI models are starting to capture how the
brain responds to what we passively watch or hear, but how brain activity *drives intelligent behaviour* remains
largely unknown. The [Digital Brain Project](https://www.digitalbrainproject.org/) sets out to collect the data needed
to model it.

Teams around the world record the brain activity of people playing interactive games inside brain scanners — a few
participants each, over many sessions, to build dense individual maps of intelligent behaviour. The recordings are
shared in a common format and pooled into an open, de-identified dataset of some 15,000 hours, used to train a digital
model of the human brain that is then tested on cognitive and reasoning benchmarks.

The project is a US$15 million fund coordinated by the Rothschild Hospital Foundation, in partnership with the
Université de Montréal and sponsored by Meta. Its first cohort brings together nine teams across Europe and North
America — among them Momchil Tomov's at Boston College, where I am a postdoc — alongside contributing labs such as
Courtois NeuroMod.
