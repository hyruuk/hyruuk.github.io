I study minds where they come alive: in the middle of a game, on a dance floor, in a dark cinema.

Most of my research puts people inside brain scanners — fMRI and MEG — while they play videogames, to watch how a brain
learns a new world, gets absorbed by it, and sometimes drifts away. Then I compare those brains with artificial agents
that play the same games.

I am a postdoc at Boston College in Momchil Tomov's lab, where we take part in the
[Digital Brain Project](/projects/digital-brain-project/): an open, international effort to record brains during games
and train a foundation model of the human brain. Before that came a PhD with Karim Jerbi and Lune Bellec at Université
de Montréal (*from brain flux to psychological flow*), a postdoc building the Courtois NeuroMod Super Mario dataset,
and a research stay with Rui Ponte Costa in Oxford.

Outside the lab, but never far from it, I co-founded the art-science collective
[Kairos ~ Hive](/projects/kairos-hive/), where brain and body signals shape images, sounds and stories, and I play
techno, psytech and drum & bass as [Xanarelle](/projects/xanarelle/).

Most of what I build is open source; the data I work on is shared with everyone.
